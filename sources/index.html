Sources & methodology

This section lists the material used throughout the archive and identifies what kind of evidence each source represents. Primary sources, academic research, community research, testimony archives and preservation records are labelled separately.

How sources are labelled. This archive distinguishes between primary sources, academic research, community research and datasets, first-person testimony, preservation records, and public-discourse material. Each category is labelled so that different forms of evidence are not treated as interchangeable.

Official company sources Academic & peer-reviewed research Public statements Community research & datasets Accessibility research First-person testimony archives Preservation records Affiliation sources

Methodology

Material is organised by source type so that primary sources, research, community datasets, first-person testimony, preservation records and public-discourse material are not treated as interchangeable. Original sources are linked wherever available, and claims are presented according to the type of evidence supporting them.

Corrections policy

This archive is intended to remain updateable. Factual corrections, additional primary material and source updates may be added as new evidence becomes available. Git history preserves previous versions of the archive.

Community research & datasets

Community research & datasets Social-media post (X) · SRC-0001

Large-scale #Keep4o public-discourse analysis

Computational content analysis of 61,846 public #Keep4o posts on X collected through the platform’s official API, covering August 2025 through March 2026.

The analysis examines themes including:

  • platform power and model lifecycle decisions
  • model value and interaction experience
  • trusted relationships
  • substitution failure / non-equivalence
  • demands for direct access and accountability
  • opposition to stigmatizing or pathologizing users

Community / computational research. Not presented here as peer-reviewed.

View source x.com

Accessibility research

Accessibility research Report (PDF) · SRC-0002

GPT-4o Accessibility Impact Survey

Structured accessibility-impact research examining how people with disabilities, chronic illness, neurodivergence and mental-health conditions reported using GPT-4o, changes in functioning during stable access, and the effects of routing or attempted substitution.

Methodology described by the authors includes structured survey questions, screening and attention checks, hierarchical regression, chi-squared tests and Pearson correlations.

The study reports findings concerning:

  • GPT-4o as an accessibility or cognitive-support tool
  • changes in reported functioning during stable access
  • attempted substitution with other models
  • effects of routing
  • human connection and daily functioning

Findings are the authors’ own reported results. The PDF report is the primary source for the findings and methodology; the X post is the public announcement.
